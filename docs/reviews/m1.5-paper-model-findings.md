# M1.5 paper model: findings

*2026-11-26. Milestone M1.5 of `docs/design.md` (draft 2, §12): before the core is built, the reference
project's frame was written as an AluKit project document against the draft schema, by a throwaway
converter from its existing hand-built model. This file is the public summary. The document, the
converter, the per-body list, the gap list and the hand check need the reference project's specifics,
so they are in `internal/paper-model/`, which is gitignored and not published. This file changes
nothing else in the repo.*

**Provenance:** *measured* (computed by the converter or by hand, and cross-checked); *read* (from
the design); *inferred* (reasoning).

## 1. Result

- **Every body fits; most only awkwardly.** The reference frame has **254 bodies** (*measured*):
  - **88** are expressed cleanly;
  - **166** only *awkwardly*: in the document, but only through a number worked by hand from other
    geometry, or with a relation the record states (a fastening, a contact, a link to a hole layout)
    lost;
  - **none** is missing.
- **Most of the awkward ones are one detail.** 124 of the 166 are the threaded rods and their crush
  sleeves that fasten a steel seat to a hollow steel beam. Repeated hardware dominates the body count.
- **The end-plane rules reproduce the record's cuts** (*measured*, by hand for nine bars and by a paper
  derive of every bar). Each matches the record's drawn length within the record's own rounding. The
  rules exercised:
  - §4.5's butt (the placed bounding box, the first face crossed);
  - splices and `extend_mm`;
  - angled butts through a bar and through a body.

  Two constructions matched only through hand-placed planes or a hand-worked trim (P1), so they are not
  evidence for the rules.
- **The record disagrees with itself.** Its bill orders two spliced diagonals node to node, and a set
  of struts by an estimate; its drawing does neither. The rules side with the drawing, so the
  acceptance test must name which record length it compares with (section 4).

## 3. Schema changes proposed

Ranked by impact. **Major** means a body is in the document only through a hand-worked number or with
a lost relation, or an acceptance test (§13) cannot pass. **Minor** is a clarification or a small field.

### Major

- **P1. An end plane taken from another bar (§4.5).** Affects 27 bodies.
  - **The problem.** A butt plane comes only from the side faces of the through bar's infinite prism.
    So three common details cannot be written:
    - a bar bearing at an angle on another bar's **end** face;
    - an end **flush** with another bar's cut end;
    - an end stopped by a face its centre line misses. One beam end validated only because its centre
      line happened to pass a few millimetres inside the through member's depth.
  - **Proposed.**
    - `butt.through` may name another bar's end (`"b7.b"`). Allowed only when that end's provider
      depends on placement alone (the default, an explicit plane, a splice, or a side-face butt).
      Stage 3 then runs in two sweeps and still never iterates.
    - `ends.<x>.plane` gains `{ "of": "<bar>.<end>" }` (a flush end) and
      `{ "face": { "bar", "face" } }` (another bar's face plane).
    - Two golden cases: an end bearing on an end face at an angle, and a flush end.
  - **v1 exclusion within it.** A mitre square to another bar (a shoe under a strut). `extend_mm`
    covers it for now.
- **P2. Bolted joints through more than two bodies (§4.5 `face`).** Affects 13 bodies. A bolt through
  strap, packer, beam, packer, strap; or angle, wall, wall, angle, block.
  - **The problem.** `face` takes two bars, and a packer cut to length cannot be both a bar on the
    cut list and a stack layer.
  - **Proposed.** `bars: [b1, b2, …]`, ordered head to nut, with contact or a declared gap checked for
    each adjacent pair (`gaps_mm`). One fastener per station passes every layer, and the grip sums
    every layer, both walls for a hollow bar.
- **P3. Fasteners cut from bar stock (§4.5, §5.3).** Affects 63 bodies. Threaded rods cut to length
  fasten a joint, but `fastener` is one catalog fastener per station, so the rods must be placed as
  separate bars that nothing links to the joint.
  - **Proposed.** `fastener: { "bar": "<threaded-rod item>", "len_mm": <expr> }`. Each station then
    adds a cut piece to the cut list (nested in stock like any bar) and carries the stack.
- **P4. Crush sleeves inside a hollow section (§4.5).** Affects 67 bodies, plus sleeves the record does
  not draw. A sleeve sits inside a tube's bore around each bolt, cut to the bore.
  - **Proposed.** A stack entry `{ "item": "<tube item>", "inside": "<hollow bar>", "len_mm": <expr> }`,
    on the cut list. A connector's `mounts` may list such pieces.
- **P5. A stock rail's holes are phased from its factory end (§4.7, §3.2).** Affects 2 bodies, and
  through them every rail bolt and seat-rod station.
  - **The problem.** §4.7 centres the holes by default. A rail sold in fixed lengths and cut at one end
    keeps its factory layout, so for such rails the default puts the holes in the wrong place. §4.7
    also names only `E_a_mm`.
  - **Proposed.**
    - The stock offer carries `factory_E_mm`.
    - The bar takes `holes: { "factory": "a" | "b" }`; `E_a_mm` and `E_b_mm` become overrides.
    - The centred layout applies only to `holes: { "drilled": true }`.
    - Nesting keeps a factory end on every piece.
- **P8. Stations from a selection of rail holes (§4.7).** Affects 124 bodies. Stations at every other
  hole, plus the first, the last and both beside a joint, have to be copied by hand as offsets.
  - **Proposed.** `rail_holes: { "along", "every": 2, "plus": ["first", "last", "joint"] }`.
- **P14. Loose BOM lines (§4.7). Acceptance test 4 needs it.** Consumables, sets and kits the model
  does not place have no entity.
  - **Proposed.** `parts` entries may be `{ "item", "qty", "unplaced": true, "why" }`: counted and
    priced, not drawn.
- **P15. Price bands on catalog prices (§4.11). Acceptance test 3 needs it.** The record spreads every
  catalog-priced line into low, likely and high, but an offer has one price, so the record's raw low
  and high totals cannot be reproduced.
  - **Proposed.** `price_band: { "<supplier | tag:… | kind:…>": { "low_pct", "high_pct", "src", "why" } }`,
    applied to each line's likely price. A valued field on the offer wins.

### v1 exclusions

- **P18. Project items of kind bar or connector (§4.8).** A project-local catalog by path (§3.1)
  already covers a pad strip and fastening recipes. The skill should say so.
- **P20. A part's own mounting hardware (carriage screws into a payload plate).** The payload is out
  of the frame's scope; the screws enter as loose lines (P14).

## 4. What looks wrong in draft 2 now

1. **The rail-hole default (§4.7).** Centring the holes is right only for a rail drilled to order. For
   a stock rail cut at one end it is wrong (P5).
2. **Acceptance test 1 (§13) does not say which record length.** The record's drawn and billed lengths
   differ by more than 0.5 mm on three lines, and the drawn geometry is right each time. The record
   also draws angled ends as square prisms from the centre-line point, while AluKit's `cut_mm` is the
   long point. The test should name the drawing as the oracle and compare `centre_mm` for angled ends,
   or list them as exceptions.
3. **Acceptance test 3 (§13) cannot pass without P15.**
4. **"Planes depend only on placements" (§4.5) holds for every construction found, except P1.** P1 needs
   a controlled two-sweep exception.
5. **Symmetric sections admit several equivalent rolls (§4.4, §2).** Canonical form should pick the
   smallest, or two documents of the same frame differ (*inferred*).
6. **The §12 M1.5 row and §15 (M9) say the converter is kept in the reference project's repository.**
   It lives in this repo's gitignored `internal/` for now.

## N1: a traced section's symmetry

### 1. Twice the stated local uncertainty

`hasSymmetry` needed every outline vertex's reflection to lie on the outline within 0.1 mm, M1's
tolerance for an exact outline (**read**). A traced outline cannot meet that: PF40-02's trace
misses its own reflection by up to 0.285 mm, though its grooves, cores and bounding box are
symmetric as the catalogue draws them (**measured**). So `mirror` refused the 40 × 80, and every
part on it, and `reverseBar` would have too.

- **The rule:** an outline is symmetric across a line when each vertex's reflection lies on the
  outline within `max(0.1, 2 × local_mm)`, where `local_mm` is how far any one edge may lie from
  the section's on its own. Each of the two edges compared may be off by that much, either way, so
  two traced edges that are one edge of the real profile can lie up to twice it apart.
- An exact or generated outline states no uncertainty and keeps 0.1 mm. A trace never judges
  more closely than that (a trace stating 0.03 mm is held to 0.1).
- **Source:** Alt, Mehlhorn, Wagener and Welzl, ["Congruence, similarity, and symmetries of
  geometric objects"](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF02187910), *Discrete &
  Computational Geometry* 3 (1988). Exact symmetry is ill-posed for measured points, so they define
  approximate (ε-) symmetry: a symmetry that maps each point to within ε of a point of the set. The
  ε here is the tolerance above, taken from the section's own stated uncertainty, not a guess.
- **Other options weighed:** symmetry declared by the catalog (a field per section) would be one
  more hand-kept fact for an importer to get wrong, and an exact section would need it too.
  Mirroring by the section's frame, ignoring the outline, would mirror an unequal angle as itself
  and buy the wrong item. Precedent agrees with judging the profile: Inventor's Mirror Components
  reuses a part only if it "must be symmetric according to its current symmetric plane to be
  suitable for reuse", and warns that "asymmetric reused components can be positioned incorrectly"
  ([Mirror Components reference](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2026/ENU/Inventor-Help/files/GUID-7B19CA69-8D14-40DA-9543-115A8AFE6336.htm)).
  SolidWorks offers a copy of a component or an opposite-hand version, a copy of an asymmetric
  part being no true mirror
  ([mirror types compared](https://help.solidworks.com/2024/English/SolidWorks/sldworks/c_MirrorComp_compare_mirror_types.htm)).
- **What it gives** (**measured**, `recheck.test.ts`): the square traced profiles have all four
  mirror lines, PF40-02 its two centre lines, and the face-plate PF30-01F only its vertical line.
  The exact outlines are unchanged.

### 2. The local uncertainty is 1.5 pixels, measured

The traced sections stated "±half a pixel locally", the 50 % contour's own error. That understated
it. Four catalogue pages draw a raster under a vector outline of the same profile, which gives a
ground truth. The new `import_forseti.py check-traces <catalogue PDF>` traces each raster as the
importer does, calibrated on the vector outline's own groove dimensions, and measures the farthest
the trace lies from the vector outline, either way: 1.11, 0.67, 1.45 and 1.23 px for PF40-00,
PF40-01, PF45-12 and PF40-03 (**measured**). A raster places an edge, and a sharp corner most, by a
pixel or more, and a trace follows its image.

- `TRACE_LOCAL_PX = 1.5`, the worst rounded up to the half pixel, in the importer's reviewed tables.
  `check-traces` exits 1 if a page ever exceeds it.
- The eight traced files are regenerated. Their notes say the new number. Their outlines are
  unchanged, because the uncertainty does not move the trace. Their groove ranges (`groove_dims`
  low and high) widen with it: PF40-02's hook 14.00–14.66 becomes 13.72–14.94 (**measured**). No
  core code reads those ranges (**read**).

### 3. Stated as data

A section file states it as `from.uncertainty: { overall_mm, local_mm }`. `overall_mm` is how far
every edge may be off together (a scale, a stroke's margin), and `local_mm` is how far one edge may
be off on its own. The core reads only `local_mm`, never the note's prose.

- It is optional in the schema, because a lock pins older sections that lack it. `catalog check`
  requires it of a traced section (`traced-uncertainty`, an error), so a built-in catalog cannot
  ship a trace that does not say.
- A traced section with no uncertainty is held to 0.1 mm, as an exact one is: the stricter reading.

## N1: advice that works

### 4. Advice is tried before it is given

"b1 is not mirrored, …; include b1" led to "forseti:PF40-02 is not symmetric …": following the
advice was refused in turn (**measured**, the re-check). The advice is now a typed refusal
(`Include`) carrying the entities it asks for. `mirror` runs as `advised()`: on an `Include`, it
tries the mirror again with them, and gives the advice only if that mirror goes through.

### 5. The advice names the whole chain

Including b13 may itself need b1 (b14 stands on b13, which stands on b1). The retry follows each
further `Include`, and the advice names them all, in one step: "b14/a: b13 is not mirrored, and no
bar stands at its image across x = 400; include b13 and b1" (**measured**). Mirroring the three goes
through.

### 6. Otherwise, it says why

When including the entity is refused for another reason, the refusal says that reason and advises
nothing: "b2/a: b1 is not mirrored, and no bar stands at its image across x = 400; mirroring b1 with
it is refused too: b1: test-open:L-76.2x50.8x6.35 is not symmetric and has no other hand, so its
mirror image is another item". Where two bars stand at the image, it says "remove all but one"
instead of advising a third.

## N3: a hole in its own reference

### 7. Every message names a written hole as it is written

The groove check named a hole written "20 from end a's cut" as "60.00 from design point a", the
distance derive measures from. A resolved hole now keeps what it was written as
(`HoleOp.written: { from, at }`; the derive output does not change). Every message about a hole
places it with `holePlace`: the groove check's, the repeated-machining check's, and the off-piece
check's ("the tap M6 20.00 from end a's cut on face -x, through: …"; "the Ø7.00 hole on face +y,
1.00 mm from end b's cut, reaches 2.50 mm past end b's cut face").

### 8. A generated hole is placed from design point a

A connector's or a fastening's hole has no written reference. It reads from design point a, as it
did.

## N5: no twin from Mirror

### 9. A bar whose image already stands is not copied

Mirroring b9 (a shelf rail across the frame's middle) made b18 exactly on it, reversed: one bar and
two brackets more in the BOM, and nothing said (**measured**, the re-check). A copy of a bar onto a
place a bar already fills is never what is meant.

- Before copying, `mirror` asks what stands at each bar's image (`standing`): the bar itself, when
  the plane maps it onto itself, or another bar of the same section and placement. Such a bar is
  **not copied**. It is recorded in `info.there` (`{ b9: "b9" }`, `{ b11: "b12" }`), and the set's
  references land on it: a part on b9 mirrored with it lands on b9, at the mirrored station.
- A mirror that would make nothing (b9 alone) is refused: "b9 is its own image across x = 500: the
  mirror would make nothing, and a copy would stand on it". The same goes for "b11's image across
  x = 500 is b12, already there".
- Precedent: Revit warns that "there are identical instances in the same place", which double-counts
  them in schedules
  ([Autodesk forum](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/revit-ideas/reveal-duplicate-items-in-view/idc-p/7462463)),
  and Tekla's clash check reports "two identical objects completely overlap"
  ([clash types](https://support.tekla.com/doc/tekla-structures/2022/mod_about_clash_types)). Both
  tools find the twin after the fact. Mirror can avoid making it at all.
- **Options weighed:** refusing whenever any image coincides would refuse the useful case, a part
  mirrored along with its bar onto the bar itself. Copying with a warning would keep the BOM wrong
  until someone acted. Skipping the copy, saying so, and landing references on what stands there
  keeps the step useful and the model right.

### 10. Holes need their images on the bar that stays

A bar not copied gains nothing. If its holes' images are not already on it, the mirror would leave
the model asymmetric while saying it mirrored, so it is refused: "b9 is its own image across
x = 500, but its holes' images are not on it, and a mirror adds none to a bar: leave b9 out, or add
them to it" (`holesThere`). Symmetric holes pass.

### 11. The words say it

The step reads "mirror b9 and p1, making p2; b9 is its own image" and "…; b11's image is b12". A
run holding a bar already there is refused ("the run has no copy to make; leave rX out").

## N5: two bars in one place

### 12. A check warning, however it was made

A twin can come from a paste, a duplicate typed wrong, or an older mirror. The check warns
(`coincident`) on a bar that stands where another stands: the same section, the same line (its
design points the other's, either way, within `tol.len`), and the same body (every vertex of its
placed outline on the other's, within the section's tolerance, xc 1). The warning goes on the later
id and names the first: "b13 stands where b9 does: the same section on the same line, one body in
one place, so the BOM counts it twice; remove one of them". Where either bar is context, the BOM
clause is left out. A bar on the same line but placed beside the other is not a twin. The bars are
swept in order along x, so only neighbours are compared (**inferred** cheap; the lattice's check is
unchanged in the comparison below). The precedent is xc 9's.

### 13. An order warning

Two bars in one place buy one bar too many, so the order warns: rule `coincident`, default `warn`.
As with `unconnected` (review-fixes-joins decision 9), it quotes the check's line alone (N8's
rule). The cart's hand calculation gains the rule in its table.

### 14. One problem

The problem list keeps the check's warning and the order's reason as one problem, "Two bars in one
place at b13", from the check and the order.

## Follow-ups from the app round

### 15. Machining problems titled by a structured reason (N10, in the core)

The app round fixed N10 by matching the end of each machining message ("off the piece, …") to pick
a title, falling back to "Machining to check". That breaks silently when a check's wording changes.

- A diagnostic may carry a `reason`. Each machining check gives one from `MACHINING_REASONS`:
  `off-piece`, `tap-in-groove`, `repeated`, `no-cores`, `off-core`, `core-fit`, `fit-unchecked`.
  "Past a cut" is `off-piece`: the hole reaches past a cut face.
- The problem list's title table is keyed by reason, and its type takes every reason, so a check
  cannot add one without its words. A machining diagnostic with no reason (an older project's
  saved answer) reads "Machining to check".
- The unpriced-machining order reason (`missing-price`) carries `services: { of, count }`, and the
  problem list words it from those, not by parsing "no price for N machining services".
- The text matching is removed (`machiningTitle`, the `SERVICES` pattern). Tests title a reworded
  message the same, and give the old words with no reason the generic title.
- The `reason` is serialized wherever diagnostics are (`alukit check --json`, `derive --json`,
  `GET /api/check`), so an agent reads it as the app does (CLI parity).

### 16. A step says one outcome per end (N2, in the core)

The step and Undo said "mirror b17, making b20; b20.a and b20.b left open, joined at 2 ends":
`mirror`'s describe listed the ends whose joints it could not land, and the step's autoConnects
then joined them (**measured**, the re-check).

- A command's phrase may now be a sentence with the ends it leaves open or joins (`Said`).
  `mirror` gives its open ends, and `autoConnect` gives the ends it joins. The step puts them
  together (`joinPhrases`): an end any command of the step joins is not said to be left open. The
  open ends that remain come last: "mirror b13, making b14, joined at 1 end; b14.b left open". This
  is the app's N2 rule (xa 3): an end is open only if nothing in the whole step joins it.
- At a corner, the through bar's own end there is joined too: the butt covers it, and derive claims
  it. `autoConnect`'s answer now names it (`covers`), and the arrangement endpoint's corner plan
  carries it, as that plan is defined as autoConnect's answer. A test holds the step's joined ends
  to the dry run's `effects.claimed`.
- `alukit do mirror` alone still says "b14.a and b14.b left open": nothing joins them.

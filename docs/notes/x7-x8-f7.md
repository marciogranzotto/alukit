# X7, X8, F7: a quick connector's cross hole and head, and far-face heads

*2026-11-27. Closes gap X7 of the round-3 acceptance, which failed the alternative frame's third run
(the decided frame's sixth run met it too, as its F5, and passed), and two gaps found beside it in
F2b's head seats: a connector's own head was not seated, and a domed head was checked as a cylinder
(X8, the decided frame's F6); a far-face head was seated where F2 had refused the hole (F7). Each is
fixed at its root with failing tests written first and a golden case worked by an independent hand
script (G46–G48). The design text follows (marked *2.3*, listed in its changelog under "After the
round-3 acceptance"; open calls from 52). Labels: **verified** (run, with how), **read** (from the
code or a document), **inferred**.*

**Failing first** (**verified**): `src/core/derive/x7-x8-f7.test.ts` was written against the code
before this change, with the test catalog `test-qc` and the fixtures G46–G48: 13 of its 14 tests
failed, the one passing being the F7 case's plain seat (the far-face head on a square hole, already
right). The schema's refusal of positions on a butting-side hole was set aside for the run, so the
X7 tests reached derive and failed there, not at loading. The file's three later tests (the Forseti
item end to end, `qty` above one, `dims` on a recipe) were written with the catalog data and the
`qty` diagnostic, after the fix, not before it.

**Golden values** (**verified**: the unit suite, and a dump of every fixture's diagnostics, butt
seats, machining and hardware before and after, 149 cases): no golden value of G1–G45 moved, nor any
fixture's diagnostics, the cart's or the base frame's outputs. The one difference is G43's head under
the lips, which now also reports its room (depth 2.05, side 0.75); no expected file pins it.

## X7: an access hole at its connector's core

**The case.** A post on a rail joined by Forseti's quick connector (the shop's "Conector Rápido
90°", the catalogue's "Conector Universal", CNX0-01, p. 24): a pin in the post's core whose head
sits under the rail's groove lips, held by a cross bush in a Ø18 hole drilled into the post's side
over the core, 25 from the end. A 40x80 or 80x80 post takes one per core. **The cause** (**read**,
`items.ts` and `hardware.ts` before this change): `requirementIssues` refused `at_mm` and `at_holes`
on a hole in the butting bar's side ("takes none"), and `requires` drilled every such hole on `+y`
at the centre line. So the only way to say two connectors was to write the hole twice, which put
both in one place (the duplicate-machining error), or once, which drilled one hole for two pins.
The Forseti item CN40-01 carried neither its hole nor its head, so every frame restated them.

1. **A butting-side hole takes positions.** The schema accepts `at_mm` and `at_holes` on it, as on
   every other requirement; the positions are read in the butting bar's section from its box
   centre (§3.4's frame), as an end tap's are. With none, the hole stays where draft 2.3 put it:
   `+y`, the centre line, so no project moves.
2. **Over the position, from the nearest face.** A cross hole serves the core it is over, so it is
   drilled square to a face of the box and offset across that face by the position's coordinate
   along it. The face is the nearest to the position (the shortest hole that reaches it), ties
   within `tol.len` going to the first of `+y`, `-y`, `+x`, `-x`, so a centred 40x40 keeps `+y`.
   The vendor gives the hole's size and its distance from the end, not the face (**read**: the
   shop page and the machining article), so the face is a call (52), and a requirement cannot name
   it in v1.
3. **It must reach its position.** A blind hole shallower than its position's distance from that
   face holds nothing over the core: a `connection` error naming the face, the distance and the
   depth, and no hole. A through hole always reaches.
4. **A position off the end** is the same `connection` error as an end tap's (one per
   requirement, listing the positions), and makes no hole.
5. **The duplicate check still applies.** Two holes at one place (two requirements with no
   positions, or a recipe that restates its connector's hole) are still one op made twice, a
   `machining` error: the place key holds the face and the offset, so holes on different faces or
   offsets are different places.

**A connector in a fastener set.** Positions alone would make each frame restate the quick
connector's hole in its recipes. The connector's data should live once, in its item, so a recipe
that places connectors must bring their machining.

6. **A connector in a set is placed at each hole of its mount.** A recipe's mount whose set holds a
   connector (as the reference frames' two-core recipes hold CN40-01) puts one at each hole, and its
   requirements are made there, their positions moved by the hole's x and y (one with none at the
   hole itself), its own head seated there (below). It is still billed by the set (`qty` per hole).
7. **The mount's plane must be the joint's**, square to the connector's z, as `at_holes` needs:
   else its holes are no place in the butting bar's section, a `connection` error, and neither its
   machining nor its head is placed.
8. **Its own sets are not brought.** A connector in a set that has fastener sets of its own is a
   `connection` error ("write its parts into the outer connector's set"), not parts left off the
   order silently: v1 has no rule for nesting sets, and the case has not come up.
9. **A recipe does not restate its connector's machining.** It comes once, with the connector, so
   a recipe that writes the quick connector's hole beside holding CN40-01 makes it twice, the
   duplicate error of decision 5 (**verified** on the reproductions: as written, their recipes
   restate the hole and get that error; without the restatement they derive clean, below).

**G46** (**verified**: `by_hand.py`): an 80x80 post on an 80x80 sill, a recipe's Ø18 x 28 hole 25
from the end at (-20, -20) and (20, 20): face `-y`, -20.00 across, and face `+y`, 20.00 across, each
20 in from its face and 8 past its core. The same from a recipe holding the quick connector (no
position of its own) at its two holes, 2 billed; one as sold in a 40x40's core, `+y` on the centre
line; a 40x80's cores (0, ±20), faces `-y` and `+y`; a hole 15 deep, 5 short of its core, an error;
a position (50, 0) off the end and a mount off the joint's plane, an error each.

## X8: a connector's own head, and a domed head

**The case.** The quick connector's pin has a head of its own, Ø18 with a domed top, under the
rail's lips; no screw goes into a tap. **The cause** (**read**, `hardware.ts` before this change):
F2b found heads only through a screw of the chosen sets into a tap the connector makes in the
butting end (`buttingTaps`), so a connector with no tap seated nothing; written as a screw into a
tap to get a check, its head, Ø18 x 4.6, was a cylinder, whose top edge meets the chamber's taper
where it is 17.52 wide (on Forseti's c8-40 groove: lip 3.79, depth 9.99, undercut 22.01 tapering
from 7.03 deep to 12.24 at the floor), so it was refused although the domed head clears.

10. **A connector's `dims`.** A connector item takes `dims` (published dimensions under the
    standard's letters, as a fastener's), a bought connector's only: a joint recipe has no body, so
    the schema refuses them there.
11. **A head of its own** when its `dims` give `dk_mm` or `dw_mm`: its bearing diameter `dw_mm`,
    else `dk_mm`, its shank `d_mm`, its height `k_mm`. F2b seats it at the connector's place (the
    butt's own connector at the end's centre, one in a set at its hole) by the rules a screw's head
    gets: where the connector requires a `through-hole` at that place, on the far face round the
    hole F2 made; else under the lips of the near face's groove under the joint, where the shank
    must pass the opening, the lip and the head the groove's depth, and the head the chamber; with
    neither, no way through, a `fit` error. Its annulus on the lips runs from its shank out (a
    screw's from the thread's ISO 273 clearance).
12. **A headed connector counted by `qty` above one** is a `connection` error, and its head is not
    seated: a butt's connector is counted, not placed (§4.5's v1 limit), so all would stand at the
    end's centre; the message points to a recipe. Spreading the copies over the end's cores would
    be a guess about where the author meant them (call 53). Its machining is still made once per
    end, as every connector's is.
13. **What holds the pin is not checked**: the cross bush in its hole and the set screw through it
    are the vendor's design, and the hole's size is its data.

14. **A domed head** gives `rf_mm`, the radius of its top (ISO 7045's letter for a pan head's): a
    cylinder of its bearing diameter up to a rim, and a spherical cap of radius rf above it that
    rises h = rf − √(rf² − R²) to `k_mm` at the centre. Any fastener or connector may give it.
15. **The room is found exactly**, not by sampling (`headRoom`): the head's half-width less the
    chamber's, the least over its height. Along the rim the head is R and the chamber does not
    widen with depth, so the rim's top edge is the least there. Over the cap, where the chamber is
    constant the room grows with height, and where it narrows at a slope a (per side per depth) the
    room is least where the cap's slope matches it, at t − c0 = a·rf / √(1 + a²), c0 = k − rf. So
    the candidates are the rim's top edge, the taper's start, that tangent point (where it falls in
    the cap) and the apex. A head with no `rf_mm` is a cylinder, its top edge the one candidate, the
    check draft 2.3 made, with the same words.
16. **Derive reports the room** under the lips, with each head: `depth_room_mm`, the groove's depth
    less where the top reaches, and `side_room_mm`, the least room per side. A head on a far face
    reports none.
17. **The quick connector's head** (**read** and **verified**, decision 22): Ø18 (A), neck 7.8
    (B), 4.48 high as drawn (a 2.01 rim and a 2.47 dome), its top a sphere of radius 17.5. The
    model's cap of radius 17.5 over Ø18 rises 2.49 over a 1.99 rim, 0.02 from the drawn split.

**G47** (**verified**: `by_hand.py`, which samples the head's height finely rather than solving
for the tangent point, as a check on decision 15): the quick connector in a 40x40 post's core on a
40x40 rail, under the top groove: it bears on 54.41 % of its Ø7.8 to Ø18 annulus, no warning; its
top reaches 3.79 + 4.48 = 8.27, 1.72 above the floor; its rim's top edge stands 5.78 deep, in the
undercut (22.01), 2.005 from the wall each side, the least anywhere (2.01 rounded). As a cylinder
Ø18 x 4.48 its top edge meets the taper where the chamber is 17.92: refused, 0.04 a side. 3 off the
groove's centre the shank misses the opening, 10 off there is no groove: a `fit` error each. Two
in an 80x80's diagonal cores: the same seat at each. A screw into a tap with the same domed head:
57.84 % of its Ø9 to Ø18 annulus, the same room; as a cylinder, refused.

**The record's figures** (**read**: the acceptance's record, not verified here): it gives the
depth room as 0.90 on the drawn slots and 1.61 on PF40-01's outline, by hand. AluKit computes 1.72
with this catalog's k 4.48; with the record's k 4.6 it gives 1.60 (**verified**: the reproduction
X8b with `rf_mm` added), 0.01 from the record's outline figure, which reads the groove's depth off
the outline rather than from the groove type. The 0.90 is not reproduced: it reads the slot's depth
off the record's own drawing (**inferred** from its label), where AluKit reads the groove type's
9.99.

## F7: a far-face head on the hole F2 makes

**The case.** A brace screwed to a plain block through its side, the screw running 30° off square
to the side. **The cause** (**read**, `hardware.ts` before this change): F2 drills a side hole
square to its face and refuses a screw off square (a `connection` error, no hole); F2b, run after
it, seated the screw's head on the far face anyway, where the end's normal (not the screw's axis)
met it, and reported it at 100 % (**verified**: the reproduction's output with the code before this
change shows the head on `b1 +x` beside F2's error).

18. **The finding is right, in part.** Seating a head where F2 made no hole is a bug: no screw
    passes, so there is no head to seat. The direction is not a separate bug: F2 makes a side hole
    only when the screw is square to the face within `tol.ang`, and then the end's normal and the
    screw's axis differ by at most that, so they reach the far face at points within its tangent
    times the bar's depth (under 0.01 mm). It mattered only for the refused holes.
19. **A far-face head is seated only where F2 made the hole, on that hole's axis.** F2 records each
    side hole it makes (by end and position, with the hole's entry point and axis), and F2b seats
    a far-face head only at a recorded one, where that axis leaves the bar. A connector's own head
    on a far face follows the same rule.
20. **F2's error stands alone** where it refuses the hole: no `fit` diagnostic beside it, and no
    head in derive's output.

**G48** (**verified**: `by_hand.py`): an M8 x 110 (dk 13) up through a Ø9 hole in a plain 80 x 80
block into a bush in a 40x40 post's core: the hole at 100, the head on the block's bottom face, 100 %.
A brace square to the block's side: the head on the opposite side, 100 %. A brace rising 30° onto the
side: F2's error, no hole, no head (draft 2.3 seated one at 100 %, where the screw's line would have
left the block by its bottom at y -29.28, not by the far side). Through a 40x40 rail: 46.05 % on its
bottom face's lips, a `fit` warning.

## The quick connector in the Forseti catalog

21. **Layer 2, by hand.** The connector's hole and head are not in the shop's API data, so they go
    in a family file, `catalogs/forseti/families/quick-connectors.json`, which the importer never
    touches, each field with its source: the hole's Ø18 and its 25 from the end from the shop page
    ("Necessita furo de … 18mm (40×40, 45×45 e 50×50) com distância de 25mm da face do perfil ao
    centro do furo", read 2026-11-26), its depth 28 from the machining article ("Ø18mm x 28mm -
    Base 40 - 40x40 Leve, Básico e Fechado, 40x80 e 80x80", read 2026-11-27); the head's Ø18 (A)
    and neck 7.8 (B) from p. 24's table for CN40-01.
22. **The head's height and dome are estimates.** The table prints neither, so they are measured
    on the page's vector drawing of the pin, scaled by its printed ØA (18): a 2.01 rim and a 2.47
    dome, 4.48 in all, the dome a circular arc of radius 17.51 (rms 0.012 mm). A second reading of
    the same paths, made independently, gave the same: 4.48, 2.01, 17.51 (rms 0.011 mm), an
    ellipse fitting worse (rms 0.061), so the dome is a spherical cap (**verified**, twice). The
    drawing's other diameters stand up to 0.22 over their printed values (ØB drawn 8.02 for 7.8,
    ØD 11.19 for 11), so `k_mm` is marked `est` with 4.3 to 4.7. The record's reading from a
    raster, 4.6, is inside that range and gives 1.60 of depth room (above).
23. **The importer names it.** The importer gave every connector of the rest of the shop the note
    "no mounting model yet", which the family file now contradicts for CN40-01. The note is the
    importer's output, so the importer changes, not the generated file: a reviewed table,
    `MODELLED_CONNECTORS` in `tables.py`, names the connectors layer 2 models with their catalogue
    pages, and the build lists those pages in the item's `docs` and keeps only the `fits` part of
    the note (**verified**: the catalog rebuilt from the cached inputs differs from the committed
    one in CN40-01's `docs` and note only). The built-in catalog's test now requires the note only
    of connectors with neither `mounts` nor `requires`.
24. **Only base 40.** The shop's product covers bases 30 to 50; the other bases' hole depths and
    heads are not read, so they keep "no mounting model yet".

## Diagnostics before and after

25. **Nothing moved** (**verified**: the dump of decision "Golden values" above, every fixture and
    each golden case's patches): G43's head under the lips reports its room besides; no diagnostic,
    op or hardware line differs. The joint test that pinned the schema's old refusal ("takes none")
    now expects a butting-side hole with positions to load, since that refusal was X7's cause.

**The reproductions** (**verified**: each run with this code on a scratch copy). As written:
X7a and X7b (recipes that hold CN40-01 and restate its hole) get decision 5's duplicate error, and
their nested holes and heads land right (X7a: `-y` -20 and `+y` 20, two heads at 54.41 %); X8a (a
pin written as a screw with no tap) seats nothing, since only a connector's own head is seated
without a tap; X8b (the head Ø18 x 4.6 with no dome) is still refused as a cylinder; F5 and F6
(CN40-01 x 2) get decision 12's error; F7 gets F2's error and no head. Written as the fix intends
(the recipes without the restated hole; the pin as CN40-01; the dome's radius given; one CN40-01 in
each core from a recipe): every one derives clean, F5 with holes on `-y` and `+y` over the 40x80's
cores and both heads under the rail's lips at 54.41 %, 1.72 and 2.01; X8b at 56.55 %, 1.60 and
2.01. The positioned cross holes that the schema refused (F5's second try) now load and drill `-y`
and `+y`.

# X9, F8, F9: a cross hole's clear mouth, a head on the lips' underside, a profile's own groove

*2026-11-27. Closes three gaps the round-4 acceptance found: a quick connector's cross hole drilled
into a face another member covers (X9; the decided frame's run met it as F10), a head under the
lips credited with the face beside the opening instead of the lips' underside (F8; the alternative
frame's X10), and the 40x80 and the 80x80 básico carrying the 40x40 básico's groove (F9). Each is
fixed at its root with failing tests written first and a golden case worked by an independent hand
script (G49). The design text follows (marked *2.3*, listed in its changelog under "After the
round-4 acceptance"; open calls 57–62). Labels: **verified** (run, with how), **read** (from the
code or a document), **inferred**.*

**Failing first** (**verified**: the new tests and fixtures copied onto the base commit and run).
`src/core/derive/x9-f8-f9.test.ts` fails to load there (it imports the new `groove.ts`), and G49's
catalog `test-cover` is refused at loading (the base schema has no `hook_mm`, `face`, `access_mm` or
`groove_dims`). So each gap was also written as a test the base schema reads, and each failed: the
G47 joint with a 40x40 bar against the post's `+y` face drilled `+y` (expected `-y`); a quick
connector's head under a Forseti 40x40 básico's lips bore 52.88 % (expected under 40 %); the 40x80's
groove gave lip 3.79 (expected 4, as p. 17 prints it). All three pass after the change, and their
substance is in the unit file and G49.

**Golden values** (**verified**: a dump of every fixture's and golden case's diagnostics, butt seats,
heads, machining and hardware before and after, 167 cases, and the unit suite): no golden value of
G1–G48 moved, nor any fixture's diagnostics. Two expected outputs change, each for its gap:

- the cart's `expected-machining.json`: each bar line now carries `machining`, its operations in the
  cut list's words (the hand script writes them; its other files are unchanged), for X9's "the

- the Forseti end-to-end test in `x7-x8-f7.test.ts` (a 40x80 post on an 80x80 básico rail, a quick

**The acceptance frames** (**verified**: both frames and the three reproductions derived before and
after, read only). In each frame the cross holes the run listed as covered move to clear faces (six
in the decided frame, eight in the alternative frame), with no new `connection` error. Each frame

gave 36.8 % from a hook read 2.69 a side.

## X9: a cross hole's mouth clear of the other members

**The case.** A quick connector's Ø18 cross hole is drilled into the post's side over its core, and
its bush is turned in through the hole's mouth after the frame stands. In both acceptance frames,
derive put holes behind shoes, beams and ties standing against the post's faces. **The cause**
(**read**, `hardware.ts` before this change): the face was the nearest one that reaches the
position (or `+y` with none), chosen from the bar's box alone; nothing looked at the other members.

1. **What covers a mouth** (`cover.ts`). Another member's body: a bar's outline (its convex pieces)
   between its end planes, or a placed part's box. It covers when it stands in front of the
   mouth's disc (radius d/2 less tol.len), from the face out to the tool's room along the hole's
   axis; the share is the area of the disc its projection takes, found exactly (convex hull clipped
   to the slab, disc-polygon area). Any share counts: a bush needs the whole mouth. Contact alone
   was the first try and missed the alternative frame's eight holes: its shoes and beams stand 2.0
   mm off the posts' faces (**verified**: measured on the derived model at 0, 0.5, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 30
   mm). Hence the tool's room.
2. **The room** is `access_mm` on the requirement, default 30 (call 57): an estimate of a bush
   driver's or a hex key's reach held square to the face; no vendor states it.
3. **The face, positioned**: of the faces from which the hole reaches its position, the nearest
   whose mouth is clear, ties within tol.len to the first of `+y`, `-y`, `+x`, `-x`. With none
   clear, the nearest, and a `connection` error naming every member in front of a reaching face.
4. **The face, unpositioned** (call 58): `+y` while its mouth is clear, as draft 2.3 put it, so no
   uncovered hole moves; else the nearest clear face as near the section's centre as `+y` (or whose
   depth reaches the centre), ties likewise; else `+y` and the error.
5. **A named face**: the requirement's `face`, or the `face` of the mount's hole the connector sits
   at (a recipe's mount). It is kept; a covered one is a `connection` error naming who covers it and
   how much (`b3 stands in front of 100.0 % of its mouth, within 30.00 of the face`), and listing
   the faces that reach the position clear. A named face the hole does not reach from is the
   existing too-short error. `face` and `access_mm` are refused on a requirement that drills no
   hole in the butting bar's side.
6. **Whoever chose the face** means the requirement, the mount or derive. A hole the project writes
   on a bar (`ops`) is not judged: one under a part may be a fastening's, made to be covered.
7. **The machining sheet names the face.** Each bar line on the sheet carries its machining in the
   cut list's words (`Ø18.00 hole 25.00 mm from end a on face -y, 28.00 deep`), in JSON and as a
   Markdown column (call 59). The UI's machining table reads the count as before.

## F8: a head on the lips' underside

**The case.** A quick connector's head (Ø18 on a Ø7.8 shank) under a Forseti 40x40 básico's lips.
The lip's underside runs about 2.7 out from the opening's edge (13.59 to 13.72 over both hooks on
the vector outline, **verified**), and beyond it the chamber rises to 1.8 under the face. **The
cause** (**read**, `fastenings.ts`): `barFaceBearing` read the face's material at the face itself,
less the opening, which credits the head with the whole width of the face beside the opening: 52.88
%, where the lips' underside gives 36.7 %.

8. **The bearing is read just above the lip's depth** (0.1 above, the seat depth) on the section's
   outline, less its holes and the groove's declared opening (`lipBearing`, call 60). An exact
   outline gives its hooks as drawn.
9. **A groove may give its hook** (`hook_mm`, the width over both hooks; `wall_mm`, the face wall
   beyond them), on a groove type or a section's own groove; the `tslot` generator then draws the
   rise beside each hook. Both or neither; the hook lies between the opening and the undercut, the
   wall between the face and the lip.
10. **A groove with no hook** is drawn with a flat lip, as before: G47's 54.41 % stands. By hand,
    for G49's rail (opening 8.18, hooks 13.6): the band of the Ø7.8–Ø18 annulus from 4.09 to 6.80
    off the centre line, 37.22 %.

## F9: a profile's own groove

**The case.** The 40x80 (PF40-02) and the 80x80 básico (PF40-03B) are drawn as raster images; their
sections were parametric envelopes with the groove type c8-40, which is measured on the 40x40
básico's vector outline (lip 3.79). p. 17 prints the 40x80's lip as 4. **The cause** (**read**,
`build.py` and `link.ts`): a section had no way to state its own groove; the type's numbers were the
only ones, for drawing and for every judgement.

11. **`groove_dims`** (`groove.ts`) on a section (a `tslot` parameter, or a drawn or box section's
    field): any of the groove's dimensions, each a valued field with its source, and `unread`, the
    dimensions its drawing does not give. They replace the type's for every groove of the section,
    in the bar's grooves (`items.ts`) and in the envelope (`tslot.ts`). The type stays the interface:
    T-nuts and systems still fit by it.
12. **A lip with no hook.** Own geometry that gives the lip and no hook says the lip has none, so the
    type's hook does not carry over (`mergeGroove`): the 80x80 reforçado's vector outline has no
    hook where c8-40 now has the 40x40 básico's.
13. **`unread` is not borrowed.** A head under the lips whose bar's own groove leaves out a
    dimension the seat needs (opening, lip, depth, undercut, the taper where the groove has one,
    and the hook without a T-nut) is not seated or checked; check says so, one info per bar item,
    naming its connections and the section's note (call 61). The envelope is still drawn with the
    type's value.
14. **What fits stays checked**: a T-nut's fit, a tap over a groove's opening, a shank through the
    opening use the merged values, the profile's own where given.

**The readings.** Labels: printed values are **read** off the page's text layer and drawing; raster
readings are **measured** with `read-groove` (`importers/forseti/forseti_importer/raster.py`) and
calibrated against the printed values on the same image.

15. **The type keeps its source.** c8-40 is still measured on the 40x40 básico's +y groove and now
    records its hooks too (`measure_groove` finds where the material beside the opening ends just
    above the lip, and the wall down the middle of each rise). A section a type is measured on
    needs no own groove.
16. **Exact outlines measure their own**: the median over their grooves, with the spread in the note
    (at most 0.07 mm over opening, lip, depth and undercut).
17. **Raster readings** (`OWN_GROOVES` in `tables.py`, the reviewed table). `read-groove` takes the
    profile's box from the rows and columns whose dark run exceeds half the darkest (so dimension
    lines do not widen it), samples bilinearly, drops dark runs under 0.45 mm (centre lines), and
    reports the groove's raw widths and edges by `measure_groove`'s definitions, the lip taken where
    the width is half way from the opening to the undercut. The outline stroke widens the dark
    region, so each image is calibrated against the values it prints: on the 40x80's (3.6 px/mm) an
    empty width reads 0.43 narrow and an edge where material ends going in 0.22 deep; on the 80x80
    básico's (3.14 px/mm, cropped to leave its dimensions out) 0.61 and 0.52. The floor reads within
    0.05 and 0.2 of the printed depth. The ranges cover the grooves' spread and those residuals, at
    most 0.3 mm (call 62).
18. **A correction to an acceptance run's reading.** The run read the 40x80's "4,5" as its lip and
    put the head's room at 0.89. The "4,5" on pp. 17 and 20 is the corner holes' inset (as
    `CORNER_HOLES` records); p. 17 prints the lip as "4", over the top left groove. The room for a
    quick connector's head (k 4.48) under it is 10 − 4 − 4.48 = 1.52, and 1.40 for a head 4.6 high.
19. **Closed profiles** (*fechado destacável*) are drawn with their covers in the slots: the
    dimensions printed behind them are taken and the rest `unread`, so a head under their lips gets
    the info, not a number from another profile.
20. **v1 limit** (call 62): the other profiles drawn as raster images still take their series'
    groove type, measured on another profile: PF20-11, PF20-12, PF20-13, PF30-01, PF30-01C/F/R,
    PF30-11, PF45-01, PF45-11, the shop-only stems and the 50x50. Reading their images is the same
    work as the 40x80's; each needs printed values to calibrate against.

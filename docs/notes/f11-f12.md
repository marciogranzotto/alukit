# F11, F12: a cover that arrives after assembly, and a cross hole's face named on the connection

*2026-11-27. Closes two gaps the decided frame's ninth acceptance run found in X9's cross-hole check
([`x9-f8-f9.md`](x9-f8-f9.md)): a member put in place after the frame's joints are made could not
say so, so its cover over a cross hole was an error although the tool reaches the hole when the
joint is made (F11); and a cross hole's face could be named only in the catalog, on a requirement or
a recipe's mount hole (F12). Each is fixed at its root with failing tests written first and a golden
case worked by an independent hand script (G50). The design text follows (marked *2.3*, listed in
its changelog under "After the decided frame's ninth run"; open calls 63 and 64). Labels:
**verified** (run, with how), **read** (from the code or a document), **inferred**.*

**Failing first** (**verified**: the new test file and G50's project copied onto the base commit's
sources and run). `src/core/derive/f11-f12.test.ts` has 13 tests; on the base 12 fail, each on the
gap (the base schema refuses `after_assembly` and `hole_faces`, so the wall is dropped and the hole
drilled from its face with no word, or the named face is not taken). The one that passes guards what
must not change: the same wall placed with the frame is still X9's error, word for word.

**Golden values** (**verified**: a dump of every fixture's and golden case's diagnostics, ops, butt
seats and heads, hardware, recipes and cut-list machining, 175 cases, before and after): nothing
moved. The unit suite passes whole (83 files, 1152 tests).

**The acceptance frame** (**verified**: its document and its document with no faces named, copied
out of the run's folder and checked, read only). As written, `check` is the run's own, 28 errors, 66
warnings and 6 infos, message for message: nothing in it says `after_assembly`. With its three wall
placeholders marked `after_assembly`, the five wall-side errors become five warnings, each naming
its wall, and nothing else moves (23 errors, 71 warnings; one more info only because the marked copy
was not in canonical form). With no faces named and the walls marked, the five errors become five
warnings the same way: each hole goes in from a face only a wall covers, two of them moved off a face
a strut shoe covers. Three of those faces are the record's own; at the other two both reaching
faces are under walls, and X9's tie order picks the other one (**read**: the run's per-hole table of
the record's faces). The run's reproduction behaves the same: named, a warning; unnamed, the hole
moves from the face a frame member covers (50 % of it) to the one only the wall covers.

## F11: a member placed after assembly

**The case.** The room's walls are placeholder parts with a box, so X9 counts them as covers. Five
quick connectors can be reached only from a wall's side, and the record makes them before the frame
is set against the wall. AluKit could not write that: a named face under a wall was always
a `connection` error, and with no name derive drilled by tie order whatever covered, under a strut
shoe where the other face had only the wall in front of it. **The cause** (**read**, `cover.ts` and
`hardware.ts` before this change): every member was taken as standing when the joint is made;
nothing in a bar, a part or a connection said when a member arrives.

1. **The question is when the tool turns.** The bush's set screw is turned through the mouth when
   the joint is made. A member put in place afterwards does not stop that; it stops re-tightening,
   inspecting or undoing the joint in place later, as the run itself observed.
2. **The member says when it arrives** (call 63): `after_assembly: true` on a bar or a placed part.
   The cover belongs to the member, not to a face: one wall marked once answers every hole behind
   it, five here. Two times in all, at assembly and after it, are what the runs have needed (a wall
   the frame is set against, a panel fitted last). A boolean, as `unplaced: true` is, because it is
   one fact about the member. Nothing in it is about walls: a guard, a cover or a brace fitted last
   is the same. An unplaced line has no box and takes none.
3. **A part on a station of such a bar arrives with it**: it cannot be there before its bar. A part
   at a pose says so itself.
4. **When a joint is made.** At assembly, unless a bar it joins (its through bar, a butting bar, a
   bar of its stack) is placed after assembly; then after assembly, and every member stands then,
   those placed after assembly too. v1 does not order the members placed after assembly among
   themselves, so it does not guess that the wall comes later than the brace (**inferred**: the
   conservative reading; ordered stages would say it, call 63's alternative).
5. **The face, chosen.** Of the faces from which the hole reaches its position (or, with no
   position, `+y` and the faces as near the centre), in X9's order: the first no member covers at
   all; else the first that only members placed after assembly cover, a `connection` **warning**
   naming them, "it cannot be re-tightened once p1 is in place"; else X9's error, as before. A face
   clear for good wins over a nearer one clear only when the joint is made. With nothing marked,
   every choice and word is X9's.
6. **The face, named.** Covered by a member there when the joint is made: X9's error, which now
   lists the other reaching faces clear when the joint is made and says "until p1 is in place" of
   those only a later member covers. Covered only by members placed after assembly: the warning,
   "its mouth is clear when the joint is made, but it cannot be re-tightened once p1 is in place".
7. **Over a joint made after assembly**, a member placed after assembly that blocks says so ("p1,
   placed after assembly, stands in front of ..."), and the error ends "the joint is made after
   assembly, since b2 is placed after it", so the reason it counts is in the message.
8. **Each cover says whether its member arrives later** (`Cover.later`); which joints it does not
   block is the joint's to say. Covers are found as X9 finds them; the bodies, the room and the
   shares do not change.
9. **Nothing else reads it.** The member is drawn, counted, priced and weighed as before; a butt's
   through body is still no cover (X9's v1 limit). Pattern copies take their template's field.

**Alternatives not taken** (call 63). Named, ordered stages (`stages: [...]`, a stage per member, a
joint at its latest member's stage) say more, but need a document-level list, its edits and a place
in the app, for a need no run has shown; `after_assembly` is their two-stage case and maps onto them.
A reason written on a named face ("made before the wall") states it per hole, not per member, and
does nothing for derive's own choice, which still drilled under a shoe. A shorter `access_mm` claims
a shorter key. Dropping the walls drops the cover test at every hole.

## F12: a cross hole's face named on the connection

**The case.** The faces the record names differ end to end and hole to hole, so naming them took 8
recipes (one per pair of faces) and 5 butts split from their groups. **The cause** (**read**,
`namedFaces` in `hardware.ts`): a face came from the requirement or the recipe's mount hole, both
catalog data shared by every joint that uses them.

10. **`hole_faces`** on a `butt`, `miter`, `splice` or `pivot` (the connections with butting ends):
    per butting end, a face for every hole in that bar's side at the end, `{ "b2.a": "+x" }`, or a
    list naming the hole at each position, `{ "b2.a": [{ "at_mm": [20, 20], "face": "+x" }] }`.
11. **Keyed by position, not index** (call 64): a position in the butting bar's section from its box
    centre, where the connector's positions are read, is what derive's messages and the machining
    sheet show, and it survives a recipe's holes being reordered. A hole with no position stands at
    [0, 0]. A position matches within tol.len.
12. **Over the catalog's**: the connection's face, then the mount's hole's, then the requirement's.
13. **Judged as a named face is** (X9 5, F11 6): from a face the hole does not reach its position
    from, no hole and the too-short error; covered, the error or the warning.
14. **What no hole takes** is a `connection` error on the connection, field `hole_faces`, and the
    entry is not used: an end that is not one of the connection's; an end where its connector drills
    no hole in the bar's side; a position where no such hole stands (the error lists where they do);
    a connection with no connector. These are derive's, not the schema's: a schema refusal drops the
    whole connection on reading, which moves cut lengths, where derive keeps the joint and says what
    is wrong (design §5.1, a half-edited document still draws).
15. **Kept in step with the ends.** Its keys are references to bars, as an end in `ends` is. A
    cascade (`removeEntity`) or a variant that drops an end drops its entry, and an emptied map goes
    too. `reverseBar`, `splitBar`, `mirror`, `array` and `explodePattern` rename each key with its
    end, and so does a pattern's copy in derive. The faces and positions stay as written: they are
    read in the butting bar's section, as the connector's own positions are, which no edit turns
    (§4.5's v1 limit: a connector is not turned to suit the end), so a face kept with its position
    still names the connector's hole, and derive judges it again.
16. **Edited by `setConnection`**, a merge patch, so no new command: `alukit do <dir>
    '{"cmd":"setConnection","args":{"id":"c1","set":{"hole_faces":{...}}}}'` from the CLI, the same
    through the server and an agent. The skill's reference is regenerated (`pnpm skill`) and its text
    says how to write both fields. The app's inspector does not edit it in v1 (a parallel UI pass
    owns its files); it shows the diagnostics, and a hand edit or the command sets it.

## G50

**G50** (**verified**: `by_hand.py`, which samples each mouth on a grid against the members' boxes
and picks faces by the rules above, imports nothing from AluKit): an 80x80 post on an 80x80 sill, a
recipe holding a quick connector at (-20, -20) and (20, 20), each a Ø18 cross hole 28 deep 25 up, so
each reaches its core only from the two faces 20 from it. A 40x40 frame member stands 2 off the
post's +y face over the (20, 20) core; a wall placeholder 24.5 off its +x face is placed after
assembly. At (-20, -20): -y, clear. At (20, 20): +y is under the frame member (100 %), +x under the
wall alone: +x, a warning naming the wall. The wall placed with the frame: no face clear, +y, an
error naming both. Named +x on the connection: the warning; the wall with the frame, an error naming
it. Named +y: an error naming the frame member. Named -x for the end: -x at (-20, -20), and at
(20, 20) a hole 60 from its core, an error and no hole. The wall turned to stand off +y, the frame
member gone: +x, clear for good, over +y. A 40x40 fitted last in the wall's place: the warning names
it. The post itself fitted after assembly: its joint is made then, the wall stands, an error. Each
head bears 37.22 % under the sill's lips, a `fit` warning.

**Not done here.** Ordered stages (call 63's alternative), an inspector field for `hole_faces`, and
`after_assembly` on anything a check other than the cross hole's reads.

# X11: context, drawn and checked against, not built or bought

*2026-11-27. Closes gap X11, which an acceptance run measured: the room a frame stands in (its walls
and floor, modelled as placeholder parts so that X9's cover test and F11's `after_assembly` could
read them, [`x9-f8-f9.md`](x9-f8-f9.md), [`f11-f12.md`](f11-f12.md)) entered the BOM, the order (as
placeholder lines that block it: "a placeholder, not a part to order") and `bbox()`. A floor slab 30
thick under z = 0 moved a size check from 2230 to 2260. Nothing could say a member is context, not
part of the build. Fixed at its root, with failing tests written first and a golden case worked by
an independent hand script (G51). The design text follows (marked *2.3*, listed in its changelog
under "Gap X11"; calls 80–84). Labels: **verified** (run, with how), **read** (from the code or a
document), **inferred**.*

**Failing first** (**verified**: the new and changed test files copied onto the base commit's
sources and run there). The unit file `src/core/derive/x11.test.ts` has 21 tests, and 20 fail on the
base: its schema refuses `context`, so a marked bar or part is dropped with a `schema` error, and
nothing else follows. The 21st, that `context: false` and a context unplaced line are refused,
passes there, as a guard. G51 fails in four of its five cases; the fifth, nothing marked, passes,
and guards that a document that marks nothing derives as before. The CLI test (`alukit do` with
`setBar` and `setPart`, in `src/cli/edit.test.ts`) fails, since the marked document does not open,
and the inspector's test file fails to load (its module is new). 25 failures in all, on the base,
besides the inspector's file. The view's test (`view.spec.ts`) was not run on the base (it needs a
build); it serves G51, whose `context` fields the base's schema refuses (**inferred** to fail).

**What moved** (**verified**: a dump of every fixture's and golden case's outputs, before and after,
187 cases counting each golden variation and each variant: `derive --json`, the cut list, the BOM,
the order, the costs, the machining sheet, `check`'s checks and diagnostics, the view model's
display, `bbox()` and the GLB's nodes, materials and a hash of its meshes): **nothing**, byte for
byte. Every output gains its context fields only where something is context. The unit suite passes
whole.

**The acceptance run's frame** (**measured**: the run's document copied to a scratch folder and
derived, read only, as written and with its four room parts marked `context`, in each variant). As written, `bbox()` is 5009 × 1484.5 × 2260, and the size check `bbox().z <= 2330` passes
by 70; marked, 4900 × 1420.61 × 2230, by 100. The run's 2230 against 2260 is the floor slab's 30.
The BOM loses its four room placeholder lines (they had no mass, so the total does not move), the
unsourced group loses its four `placeholder` reasons, and derive's diagnostics are identical, every
cross hole's cover and warning included. The order stays blocked by its own other reasons.

## Sources

How CAD and BIM tools mark geometry that is drawn and used but is not part of what is built:

- **Autodesk Inventor**, a component's *BOM structure* "Reference" (**read**, [About the BOM
  Structure](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2026/ENU/Inventor-Help/files/GUID-A0A95805-CBF6-4524-8314-4AC8B53C19CF.htm)):
  reference components are "used for construction geometry or add context to a design" (its
  example: a tote added for drawing or publication); they "are ignored in the BOM", "are not
  numbered and are not directly included in quantity calculations", "are excluded from mass and
  volume calculations", and "are shown with a hidden line style in drawing views". The structure is
  a file's default, overridden for one instance in the assembly (**read**, [Set BOM Structure
  property](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2015/ENU/Inventor-Help/files/GUID-F5887558-E6FE-4DC8-968D-D5E76C77B736.htm)).
- **SOLIDWORKS**, an *envelope* component: "Envelopes (part or assembly) are ignored in the bill of
  materials and mass properties", and hidden by default in drawings (**read**, [GoEngineer, How to
  Use SOLIDWORKS Envelope Mode](https://www.goengineer.com/blog/solidworks-envelope-mode); the
  vendor's own [Assembly Envelopes](https://help.solidworks.com/2016/English/SolidWorks/sldworks/c_Assembly_Envelopes.htm)
  page renders by script and could not be read, its search excerpt says the same). The API also
  names a component property `ExcludeFromBOM` (**not verified**: only its name, in the API's index;
  its page could not be read, so its scope is not known here).
- **Onshape**: a part's property "Exclude from all BOMs", for every instance, and a BOM row's
  "Suppress from this BOM", for one instance (**read**, through its [help on the BOM
  table](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/Assembly/working_with_the_bom_table.htm) and its
  [tech tip](https://www.onshape.com/en/resource-center/tech-tips/tech-tip-exclude-parts-or-assemblies-from-the-bom)).
- **Autodesk Fusion**: the brief named an "Exclude from BOM"; none was found in Autodesk's own
  documentation. Fusion's BOM dates from its March 2024 release, its forums ask for a per-component
  exclusion, and a third-party add-in offers one (**not verified**; searched 2026-11-27).
- **IFC**: an element's `Status` in its common property set, per occurrence, `NEW`, `EXISTING`,
  `DEMOLISH` or `TEMPORARY`, which tools map to "new building" and "as-built" (**read**, [Allplan's
  mapping](https://help.allplan.com/Allplan/2019-1/1033/New/93336.htm)); and `IfcVirtualElement`,
  for "imaginary, placeholder or provisional areas, volumes and boundaries", with no quantities or
  materials, among its types `CLEARANCE` (**read**, from the IFC 4.3 documentation's
  [entity page](https://ifc43-docs.standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4x3/HTML/lexical/IfcVirtualElement.htm)
  through a search excerpt; the page itself refused the fetch).

What they agree on: such geometry is out of the BOM, the quantities and the mass; it is still drawn,
distinctly (a hidden line style, a transparent envelope); and it is marked **on the occurrence**,
with at most a default on the part (Inventor's file default, Onshape's part property). IFC keeps
apart what exists and stays (`EXISTING`) from what is no physical thing at all (`CLEARANCE`); both
are context to a frame.

## The model

1. **What is wrong is the kind.** A placeholder (§4.8) is a part still to choose and buy: a motor
   not yet picked, priced as an estimate, which rightly holds its order line. The walls and the
   floor are not to buy at all. Making placeholders not block would lose that; the room needs its
   own word.
2. **Context is a fact about the member** (call 80): `"context": true` on a bar, a placed part (on
   a station or at a pose), or a butt's through body (`{ "body", "at", "context" }`), the three ways
   a body enters the model. On the occurrence, as Inventor's instance override, SOLIDWORKS'
   envelope and Onshape's per-instance suppression mark it, because the same item is built in one
   place and context in another: an existing 40x40 frame extended with new 40x40 bars, a steel beam
   from `std` the frame bolts to. A catalog bar cannot be made a project kind without a
   project-local catalog per case. A boolean, as `after_assembly` and `unplaced` are: one fact.
3. **The word.** `context`: Inventor's own ("add context to a design"), and wide enough for both of
   IFC's cases, what exists (a wall, a bench) and what is no thing (a clearance envelope).
   `reference` would collide with AluKit's reference catalogs and its id references; `existing`
   would leave the envelope out.
4. **An unplaced line takes none** (the schema refuses it): it is counted and never drawn, so
   there is nothing to be context.
5. **A part on a context bar's station is not context unless it says so** (call 83). A bracket
   screwed to an existing beam is bought. This differs from `after_assembly`, where a part cannot
   arrive before its bar; nothing stops a new part from standing on an old bar. A pattern's copy
   takes its template's field, as every field.
6. **A connection is context when every member it joins is** (call 82): a butt's through bar (or the
   bar whose end it names, or its body, marked), its butting ends' bars and its stack; a miter's or
   splice's two ends; a pivot's base and ends; a face connection's layers, a run standing for all
   its bars. It is the context's own joint, already made (the existing bench's splice): what it
   brings (connectors, fastener sets, stacks, pieces cut from stock, recipes) is not counted, its
   machining is not listed, and a butt of it naming no connector asks for none (`butt-no-connector`
   and the order rule `no-connector` leave it out). The trap it closes: drawing an existing frame
   with auto-connect would otherwise buy the project's default butt connector for every one of its
   corners. A connection with a built member is the build's, whatever else it joins: the frame
   fixed to a wall buys its fixings.
7. **What stays the same.** Derive treats a context member as any other: it is placed, its ends
   resolve, butts bear on it, it covers a cross hole's mouth (X9) and arrives after assembly where
   it says so (F11), face connections touch it, `len()`, `cut()`, `pos()` and `dist()` read it.
   Only what derive collects is marked: `Derived.context` (the ids) and a flag on each bar, part,
   face connection, hardware use, piece and recipe that is context (`context-ids.ts`, `derive.ts`).

**Each output** (call 81):

8. **The cut list**: a context bar is not cut, so no piece, no line, no nesting, no offer, no
   `no-offer` warning and no length error; nor the pieces a context connection cuts from stock.
9. **The BOM**: context is in no group and no total and has no mass, so no `mass` warning for a
   wall that gives none. It is **listed apart**, as joint recipes are, `context: [{ id, kind, item,
   name, opt }]` (a bar, a part, or a butt's through body under its connection's id), and in
   Markdown and CSV, so a reader sees what was left out and why. The hardware of a context
   connection is not listed at all: it is not a member.
10. **The order and the costs** follow the BOM and the cut list: no line, no `placeholder` reason,
    no missing or estimated price; `cost()` and the totals likewise.
11. **`mass()`** is the BOM's total: the build's mass.
12. **The machining sheet** lists **in place** (`in_place`) what the build machines on a context
    bar, measured from the bar's own ends (it is not turned end for end, since it is not cut): the
    holes a person writes on it and what a built connection makes in it (a hole drilled in a beam
    to fix the frame to it). What a context connection makes is the context's own, there already.
    In-place machining is no supplier's service, so it asks for no price.
13. **`bbox()`** is the build's extent (call 84): context bodies, and a context connection's
    fasteners, are left out, and a context part with no box no longer makes it unmeasurable. The
    acceptance's size check is about the frame, not the room; a check about the room compares the
    frame's box with a param.
14. **`count(tag:…)`** counts entities as tagged, context or not: the tag is the user's selector.
15. **The 3D view** draws context translucent in its own neutral tone (`ViewTheme.body.context`),
    outlined, whatever its item: a context placeholder is drawn as context, and colouring by item
    keeps context's tone, since it is no item of the build. Square context bars and a context
    connection's fasteners share one translucent pair of materials made with the scene and kept, as
    the opaque pair is; an angled context bar and a context part take a material whose parameters
    the translucent parts already use. So a revision links no shader program (**verified**:
    `view.spec.ts`, "context draws translucent", unmarks a bench post and the floor in one step,
    then marks the post again, and counts zero links each time; the first problem badge a tab draws
    links the markers' programs once, whatever the model, so the test opens on a model with one).
    The camera still fits everything drawn (**read**: `scene.ts`), so a frame shows in its room.
16. **The GLB** keeps context where it stands, so the frame exports in its room: each node with
    `extras.context`, one translucent material of its own (`context, translucent`), and no SKU or
    cut length, since it is not bought or cut; a reader that wants the build alone drops the
    flagged nodes. `derive --json` flags context on its bars, parts, face connections, pieces,
    hardware and recipes.
17. **The edit path**: `setBar` and `setPart` are merge patches, so `{ "context": true }` marks a
    member and `null` unmarks it, through `alukit do`, the server and an agent alike; `setConnection`
    sets a through body's. Canonical form places the field after `after_assembly`, by the schema;
    undo restores the document byte for byte (`x11.test.ts`); the skill says when to use it rather
    than a placeholder. The inspector shows a Context checkbox on a bar and on a placed part, one
    step each; nowhere else in the app changes.

**Alternatives not taken.** An item-level kind or a placeholder field (`context` on a project item):
fails a catalog bar and an item used both ways; Inventor's file default over an instance override
would be the way to add one later. IFC's status as an enum (`status: existing | new | temporary`):
says more than any output reads, and `temporary` (a jig removed after use) would need a meaning in
the order no run has asked for. Separate exclusions per output (`exclude_from_bom`,
`exclude_from_bbox`, as a component's separate properties allow): a wall left in the BOM but out of
`bbox()` is no case anyone has, and one flag cannot disagree with itself. Leaving context out of the
GLB: loses the room the frame is checked against. A `bbox(all)` accessor: no check has needed the
room's extent.

## G51

**G51** (**verified**: `by_hand.py`, which works each value from the document and the test catalog's
numbers and imports nothing from AluKit): two 1000 posts of the test catalog's 40x40 and a bracket
on one, beside a bench marked context (its 1200 post, drilled in place for the frame with a Ø8.5
hole on +x 500 from end a, and its top rail in two 500 pieces joined by their own splice), a floor
slab 30 thick under z = 0 and a wall, placeholders, marked context. The cut list: 2 × 1000. The BOM:
two posts, 2000, 2.986 kg, and the bracket, 0.15 kg: 3.136 kg; the bench's three bars, the floor and
the wall listed apart. The order: TS40-c7 253.00 and BRK-40 8.90, 261.90, READY. `bbox()`
440 × 40 × 1000. The machining sheet: the post's hole, in place. Nothing marked: the BOM gains three bars, the
splice, its stud and the two placeholders (6.486 kg, the placeholders without mass), the order is
blocked, `bbox()` 3000 × 3000 × 2530. The floor alone unmarked: `bbox().z` 1030, the slab's 30. The
rail's first piece new: the splice joins a built bar, so it is the build's and bought, its tap in
that piece on the cut list and its tap in the other piece made in place. A post of the frame marked
context: its bracket is still bought.

**Not done here.** A butt's through body is not drawn at all in v1, so a context body is not drawn
either (**verified** on G28: its `derive --json` holds no part for the body, and `src/mesh/` draws
bars, parts and fasteners only; the design's §4.5 said "drawn and counted", now corrected as a v1
limit). A default on an item (call 80's alternative). The order of members placed after assembly
among themselves (F11's limit) is unchanged.
